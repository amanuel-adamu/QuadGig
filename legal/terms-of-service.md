# Quadgig — Terms of Service (Draft)

*This is a working first draft, written to accurately reflect how the platform actually functions as of September 2026. It is not final, legally reviewed language — treat it as a starting point for an actual lawyer to review before this goes live for real users.*

---

## 1. Who Can Use Quadgig
Quadgig is currently limited to students with a verified `.edu` email address from a participating campus. One account per person. You're responsible for keeping your login credentials secure and for all activity under your account.

## 2. What Quadgig Is (and Isn't)
Quadgig is a platform that connects students who want to offer a service ("Sellers") with students who want to hire one ("Buyers"). Quadgig is not a party to the agreement between a Buyer and Seller, does not perform the services listed, and does not guarantee their quality, timeliness, or outcome. Sellers are solely responsible for the accuracy of their listings and for fulfilling what they agree to.

## 3. Listings
Sellers may post listings describing a service, its price, and category. Quadgig does not pre-review listings before they go live. Quadgig may remove a listing at any time if it violates these terms.

## 4. Orders and Payments
When a Buyer places an order, payment is collected immediately and held by Quadgig's payment processor (Stripe) — it is not sent to the Seller yet. Quadgig retains a 10% platform commission on each completed order; the remaining 90% is released to the Seller once the order is confirmed complete.

Funds are only released to the Seller after the Buyer marks the order as confirmed. Until that point, the Seller has not been paid.

## 5. Cancellations and Refunds
An order can be cancelled by either the Buyer or Seller before it has been confirmed complete. If payment was already collected at the time of cancellation, it is automatically refunded in full to the Buyer.

Once an order has been confirmed complete by the Buyer, funds are released to the Seller and the transaction is final. Confirmed orders are not eligible for refund through the platform.

## 6. Disputes
If a disagreement arises before an order is confirmed, either party can flag it as disputed. **Being direct about a current limitation:** Quadgig does not currently have a formal mediation or arbitration process for disputes — a disputed order can still be moved to confirmed (by the Buyer) or cancelled (by either party, triggering a refund if applicable), but the parties are expected to resolve the disagreement between themselves. Contact pjk2@williams.edu if you need the platform to step in.

## 7. Reviews
Only a Buyer who has completed a real order with a Seller can leave a review for that order. Reviews should reflect an honest account of the actual transaction. Manipulating or fabricating reviews is prohibited.

## 8. Prohibited Conduct
You may not: use Quadgig for anything illegal; misrepresent your identity or a listing; attempt to take a transaction off-platform to avoid fees; harass or discriminate against another user; or attempt to circumvent the `.edu` verification requirement.

## 9. Limitation of Liability
Quadgig is provided "as is," without warranties of any kind. To the fullest extent permitted by law, Quadgig is not liable for: the quality, safety, legality, or outcome of any service listed or performed; disputes between Buyers and Sellers; or the conduct of another user, including harm arising from an in-person interaction arranged through the platform. This includes direct, indirect, incidental, consequential, and special damages.

## 10. Suspension and Termination
Quadgig may suspend or terminate an account that violates these terms, at its discretion.

Quadgig may update these terms from time to time. Continued use of the platform after a change means you accept the updated terms.

**Known gaps to flag for the lawyer conversation specifically:**
- Quadgig currently has no formal legal entity. This document uses "Quadgig" as if it's a contracting party, but without incorporation, the real liable party in a dispute would likely be the founders personally, not a separate entity. Forming one (commonly an LLC, for the liability shield) is worth discussing before real transactions go live — ideally before Phase 3's soft launch, not after
- Section 6 (disputes) describes a real, current limitation honestly rather than promising a process that doesn't exist — worth deciding whether to build real dispute handling before or after finalizing this language
- No insurance, liability waiver, or background-check language for higher-risk service categories, if the platform ever expands beyond low-risk services like tutoring
- Massachusetts governing law is a reasonable default given Williams' location, but not confirmed correct for your specific situation
- Comparable platforms (TaskRabbit) use mandatory binding arbitration clauses and explicit independent-contractor classification for sellers. Neither is in this draft — both are genuine, consequential legal-strategy decisions, not boilerplate, and worth a deliberate choice with the lawyer rather than copying blind
